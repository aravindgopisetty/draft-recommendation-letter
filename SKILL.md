# Draft Recommendation Letters

Write a credible evaluation that gives the reader a clear basis for trusting the recommender's judgment. Turn firsthand observations into an endorsement tailored to the opportunity.

## Establish the assignment

- Identify the candidate, opportunity, recipient, and recommender. Establish the recommender's role, how and when they worked with the candidate, and what they can assess directly.
- Use the recipient's prompts, criteria, language, length limits, and formatting or authorship rules when available. For a named application, verify relevant requirements on the official program or employer page when they could affect the draft and the user has not supplied them. Handle a portal questionnaire field by field.
- Label text prepared for someone else's signature as a draft for the recommender's review. If the applicable rules prohibit applicant drafting or other requested assistance, provide permitted support such as an applicant-authored factual briefing or questions for the recommender. The recommender owns the final judgments, signature, and any required direct submission.

## Gather and select evidence

- Use notes, an existing letter, a CV, and opportunity materials already supplied. Treat the CV and statement of purpose as background. Use first-person observation only for facts within the recommender's stated knowledge and attribute relevant secondhand facts when needed.
- Look for two or three examples that answer the reader's main questions when the record supports them. For each, identify the situation, what the candidate personally did, the observed outcome or change, and what it shows about readiness for this opportunity. One well-observed example can carry a short reference.
- Ask a small set of focused questions when the relationship, endorsement, or substantive evidence is missing or conflicting. If a draft is requested immediately, use confirmed facts and mark essential gaps with precise bracketed prompts. Keep unresolved facts and verification notes visible outside the letter.
- Use genuine comparisons only when the recommender supplies a relevant peer group and basis for judging it. Include scope, timeframe, or cohort size when known and helpful. Use a percentile, rank, or metric only if supplied and supportable. Track the candidate's contribution separately from team outcomes and preserve the status of publications and awards.

## Draft or revise

- **Opening.** Name the candidate and opportunity. State the recommendation and establish the relationship, observation context, and duration when known.
- **Body.** Build each paragraph around a claim the evidence can support. Show the candidate's decision, behavior, or contribution in a particular situation and explain its significance. Include the outcomes the recommender can substantiate. Connect the strongest evidence to what the reader is selecting for.
- **Closing.** Give a clear endorsement at the strength the recommender can stand behind. Offer follow-up and include the name, title, organization, and verified professional contact details when appropriate. Leave official letterhead and signature for the recommender to supply.
- Write in a natural first-person professional voice. Calibrate enthusiasm to the recommender's assessment. For revisions, preserve facts, voice, and degree of endorsement unless the user asks for a change the evidence supports. For several recommenders, build each letter around that writer's own observations and relationship.
- Use [evidence and format guidance](references/evidence-and-formats.md) when adapting to a particular opportunity, answering structured prompts, framing a comparison, or writing about growth.

## Review before delivery

Check every evaluative claim against its evidence and the recommender's vantage point. Verify names, pronouns, titles, dates, individual credit, metrics, and any claimed comparator. Check that the recommendation answers the actual prompt, respects each word or character cap, and reads as a coherent assessment. Keep unrelated sensitive details and confidential information out of the letter. Deliver the draft or requested critique in the requested format. Flag unresolved items briefly outside the letter. Cite drafting sources only in accompanying notes when useful.
