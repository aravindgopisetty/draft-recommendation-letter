# Evidence and formats

## Match the opportunity to what the recommender observed

| Opportunity | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Professional letter for a master's or MBA | A supervisor's or project lead's account of the candidate's judgment, initiative, collaboration, or increasing responsibility. Link workplace behavior to readiness for the course. |
| Academic admission | An instructor's or mentor's account of how the candidate reasons, writes, learns from critique, or contributes to a sustained project. For research programs, show how the candidate posed a question, tested an idea, or handled an ambiguous result. |
| Academic appointment | Evidence of research independence, teaching, mentoring, or service in proportion to the role's stated priorities. Distinguish the candidate's intellectual contribution from the lab's work. |
| Job reference | Examples of performance at the required level of scope, reliability, or ownership. Describe who relied on the candidate's work and what improved or got delivered. |
| Scholarship, fellowship, or award | Map a documented contribution or its impact to the published selection criteria. Explain why the result matters within the relevant field or community. |

For a free-form professional study-abroad letter, roughly one page with four or five short paragraphs and a ceiling of about 700 words is a practical fallback when the program gives no limit. Let the amount of relevant evidence guide the length. Use a committee or role-based salutation when the reader's name is unavailable. Leave dates, addresses, letterhead, and contact fields to the requirements and supplied details. [IDP India professional LOR guide](https://www.idp.com/india/study-abroad/letter-of-recommendation/professional-lor/)

## Put observed behavior on the page

These examples are fictional and illustrate the level of detail to ask for.

- **Workplace evidence.** “During our weekly dispatch reviews, I watched Leena trace repeat delays to orders that arrived after the warehouse handoff. She tested an earlier cutoff with one shift, then showed the dispatch team how to put it into practice. The shift adopted her process for the next reporting period.” This supports initiative and implementation even when no delay figures are available.
- **Academic evidence.** “In our thesis meetings, Mateo noticed that his conclusion depended on how he coded missing survey responses. He reran the analysis with two defensible treatments of the missing data and narrowed his conclusion to the pattern supported by both.” This supports independent judgment without depending on a publication or class rank.
- **Peer context.** A supervisor who can substantiate the observation might write, “Of the 18 analysts I managed over five years, she was one of three I asked to lead a client review during their first year.” This gives a meaningful comparison without implying a percentile. When no comparison is supportable, give the reader context through the difficulty of the work, the candidate's degree of autonomy, or the standard they met. ## Handle structured prompts and growth

- Answer each required prompt in order and use its own length limit. For a GMAC-style form, use the relationship field for scope of observation, the peer-comparison field for a supported comparison and example, and the feedback field for a specific incident. Use a no-basis option when appropriate and available. Leave an optional field blank if it would repeat the other answers. Use the school's own wording and limits. [GMAC Common Letter of Recommendation](https://www.gmac.com/reach-and-recruit-students/prepare-candidates-for-the-exam-classroom/common-letter-of-recommendation-lor)
- For feedback or material growth, describe what happened, what the recommender asked the candidate to change, how the candidate responded, and what the recommender later observed. Verify unknown improvement before claiming it. Address development areas when the prompt asks or the issue is relevant to the evaluation. [Academic Letter Architect methodology](https://github.com/lyndonkl/claude/blob/main/skills/academic-letter-architect/resources/methodology.md)
- Check who may write the recommendation and who must submit it. For example, Stanford Graduate Admissions requires recommendations to be written solely by the recommenders and provides a no-basis rating on its form. Use such requirements only for the application they govern. [Stanford Graduate Admissions recommendations](https://gradadmissions.stanford.edu/apply/recommendations)
